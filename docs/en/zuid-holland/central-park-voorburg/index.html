Our verdict

Dining and staying at Central Park Voorburg

For us, Central Park in Voorburg is the best place in South Holland to dine and stay the night, and it is sixth in our national list. The house is a stately manor from 1751 on the Vreugd en Rust estate, a listed monument in a park that landscape architect J.D. Zocher Jr. perfected around 1830. You are on the edge of The Hague, yet you look out over trees, water and a deer park.

Tim Bood and Hette Hettema run the kitchen, and since 2023 the restaurant has held a Michelin star. They cook classically yet in a contemporary way, with traditional techniques and intense sauces that stay fresh and lively. No showmanship on the plate, just flavour. The real trump card is the wine: host and co-owner Joey de Kruijf, an SVH Master Host, has built a list of more than 2,750 wines, which Gault&Millau named Wine List of the Year in 2023. For us that is a dangerous quality in a restaurant, unless you can also sleep there.

You can: fourteen rooms and suites, all with Hästens beds, and from the finest suites you look into the park. On sleep, Central Park scores almost the highest in our whole book, and the house is a member of Relais & Châteaux and holds a Michelin Key. The small reservation is the calendar: the restaurant is closed on Sundays and Mondays, so plan your stay for another evening. If you love wine, here you do not have to choose between one more glass and going home.

Dining at Central Park: classic cooking and a wine list of the year

The kitchen brigade is led by chefs Tim Bood and Hette Hettema, who work with whatever the season offers. Their style is classic yet contemporary, in keeping with the grandeur of the listed manor. You choose the Chef's Menu, centred on the season, or the Prestige Menu, built around the most luxurious ingredients they use. There is also a three-course lunch and an extensive à la carte menu.

The guides are full of praise. Gault&Millau describes red mullet with pork belly, smoked butter, herring roe and peas, and Veluwe saddle of venison with cherries, beetroot and pink pepper. The Michelin inspectors mention duck cooked on the carcass, with beetroot, blackberries, goose liver and a sherry jus, and grilled turbot with carrot and a mussel nage. Their verdict: no showmanship on the plate, just flavour, subtle yet outspoken. We agree.

Front of house is led by Joey de Kruijf and Sasja Moonen, who guide you through a list of more than 2,750 wines from every corner of the world. Besides the Gault&Millau title, the list has also received a Best of Award of Excellence from Wine Spectator. In good weather you dine on the sheltered terrace with its outdoor bar and view of the greenery. The restaurant is open for lunch and dinner Tuesday to Saturday.

Staying at Central Park: fourteen rooms with Hästens beds

Central Park is a boutique hotel with fourteen rooms and suites, and every one has a Hästens bed. You choose from Comfort Rooms, Deluxe Rooms, an Executive Suite, a Royal Suite and the Royal Terrace Suites. The rooms have air conditioning, a smart TV, a minibar, Nespresso and Atelier Rebul toiletries.

Our pick, without hesitation, is a Royal Terrace Suite. These are on the first floor, forty-eight square metres, romantically furnished and with a large terrace with lounge seating overlooking the park and the river. The bed is a king-size Hästens of two by two point one metres, and the bathroom has a bath and a rain shower. After an evening with Joey de Kruijf's wine list, that is exactly where you want to end up.

Breakfast, lunch or a simpler dinner can also be taken in the house's contemporary restaurant, overlooking the garden or the neighbouring deer park. On Sundays the restaurant is closed, but breakfast is served. There are packages, such as the Central Park Package and a cycling package around the Vreugd en Rust estate.

The building: the Vreugd en Rust estate, a listed manor from 1751

In 1710 a farmstead on the Vliet was given the name Vreugd en Rust, and in 1751 a new owner built the manor that still stands today. In the late eighteenth century it belonged to Hester van Staphorst, known as the richest girl in Holland. Her daughter married Jacobus Groen van Prinsterer, and their son Guillaume, later a famous statesman and historian, described life at Vreugd en Rust in his student diary. Around 1830 the family had J.D. Zocher Jr. perfect the landscape park.

In the twentieth century the house was in turn a hotel-restaurant, a Montessori school and a nursing academy. In 1987 it was sold in poor condition to chef Henk Savelberg, who opened a hotel-restaurant there in 1989. From 2014 to 2018 it was Central Park by Ron Blaauw, and it has carried on independently since. Inside you can still see the richly decorated staircase with eighteenth-century rococo stucco. Since July 2022 the house has been a member of Relais & Châteaux.

Location and surroundings: things to do around Voorburg and The Hague

Central Park lies in the Vreugd en Rust park in Voorburg, between the Vliet canal and the old village. The park, with its ponds, centuries-old trees, a deer park and the Juliana en Bernhardpark, is a green lung in the middle of the Randstad, and a stroll after breakfast is, for us, part of the stay.

Voorburg has another famous estate: Hofwijck, Constantijn Huygens' country house, which Guillaume Groen van Prinsterer saved from demolition in 1849. The Hague is around the corner, with the Mauritshuis, the Binnenhof and the Lange Voorhout, and within half an hour you are by the sea at Scheveningen. Delft, with its canals and Delft blue, is a short drive, and with the hotel's cycling package you follow the Vliet through the countryside.

Thanks to its location between The Hague, Rotterdam and Schiphol, Central Park is easy to reach by car and public transport. Voorburg station and the stations of The Hague are a short distance away.

Who is Central Park Voorburg for?

Central Park is for wine lovers, for those who appreciate classical cooking with fine sauces and for anyone seeking a romantic night in a historic house surrounded by a park. It is ideal for an anniversary or birthday, with the Royal Terrace Suite as the highlight, and for those wanting to combine a city break in The Hague with peace and quiet.

It suits those wanting to dine on a Sunday or Monday less well, because the restaurant is closed then. If you are looking for exuberant, experimental cooking, what you will mainly find here is classical craftsmanship. And if price matters, bear in mind that an evening with the wine list can add up quickly.

Good to know

The restaurant is closed on Sundays and Mondays: plan your stay for another evening and book a Royal Terrace Suite if you can.

Frequently asked questions about Central Park Voorburg

Does Central Park Voorburg have a Michelin star?

Yes. The restaurant at Central Park in Voorburg has held a Michelin star since 2023 and has 15.5 points from Gault&Millau. The hotel also holds a Michelin Key and is a member of Relais & Châteaux. The chefs are Tim Bood and Hette Hettema.

How many wines are on Central Park's list?

Central Park's wine list has more than 2,750 wines from around the world. Gault&Millau named it Wine List of the Year in 2023, and Wine Spectator gave it a Best of Award of Excellence. Host Joey de Kruijf and Sasja Moonen advise at the table.

How many rooms does Central Park Voorburg have?

Central Park has fourteen rooms and suites, from Comfort and Deluxe Rooms to an Executive Suite, a Royal Suite and the Royal Terrace Suites with a large terrace overlooking the park. Every room has a Hästens bed.

What does dinner with an overnight stay at Central Park cost?

For two, we count on about 500 euros for dinner, one night and breakfast at Central Park in Voorburg (midweek, November 2026). Wine comes on top, and with this list that can add up considerably.

When is the restaurant at Central Park open?

Central Park's restaurant is open for lunch and dinner Tuesday to Saturday. On Sundays the restaurant is closed and only breakfast is served, and it is closed on Mondays too.

What is the history of Central Park Voorburg?

Central Park occupies the 1751 manor of the Vreugd en Rust estate, once owned by the Groen van Prinsterer family. Chef Henk Savelberg turned it into a hotel-restaurant in 1989, Ron Blaauw followed from 2014 to 2018, and it has continued independently since.
