Our verdict

Dining and staying at Merlet

Merlet in Schoorl sits on the edge of the widest dune area in the Netherlands, with a sheep meadow at the door and the Schoorl dunes behind. It is a family hotel with a starred restaurant, and has been for a long time: the Van Bourgonje family started here in 1984, and a Michelin star has hung here without a break since 1998. A run like that tells us more than any review.

Chef Marco Helsloot cooks with refinement and the seasons, but looks close to home: pike-perch from the IJsselmeer, John Dory from the North Sea, Dutch wild duck and onions from a nearby farm. No flown-in luxury, just what the coast and its hinterland provide. Braised Volendam eel is one of his signature dishes. The vegetarian menu is good enough to have earned praise from the green guide. For something more relaxed, eat at Grand Café Bourgogne, where your dog is welcome too.

Merlet is third in our North Holland list, behind the two grand houses in Amsterdam. For the kitchen, that is almost unfair. The difference lies in the stay: the 28 rooms are all different, from standard rooms to suites with their own names, and some are more modern than others. So choose a royal room or suite deliberately. In return you get a pool and sauna in the basement, a peaceful setting and a price that is kind for a starred evening by the sea. The restaurant is closed on Mondays.

Dining at Merlet: a Michelin star since 1998, with the coast on your plate

The restaurant is the heart of Merlet, looking out over the sheep meadow and the dunes. It has held a Michelin star since 1998, which the house has now kept for more than a quarter of a century in a row, through a succession of chefs. Alan Pearson won the star, Wilco Berends, now chef-owner of De Nederlanden in Vreeland, retained it in 2001, and today Marco Helsloot is in charge. Merlet has been a member of Alliance Gastronomique since 2009.

The current Chef's Menu includes a langoustine bombe filled with North Sea crab salad, an onion soufflé from the Noorderhoeve farm, IJsselmeer pike-perch with Brussels sprouts and a sauce of sauerkraut and Madras spices, John Dory with porcini and a cocoa sauce, and Dutch wild duck with fennel, orange and a jus with honey and pollen. Dessert is Sicilian pistachio with lemongrass and vanilla. You choose the six-course Signature Menu or the five-course Alliance Menu, and there is a vegetarian menu that earned four radishes from the Green Guide.

Grand Café Bourgogne, in the old restaurant, is the relaxed alternative, with a seasonal menu that changes every two months and an à la carte menu. Our verdict: this is a kitchen with its own face that takes its region seriously. The restaurant serves its menus Tuesday to Sunday, and also at lunch on Fridays and Sundays.

Staying at Merlet: 28 rooms and suites on the edge of the dunes

Merlet has 28 rooms, and no two are alike: each has its own design, colours, layout and furniture. You choose from standard rooms, royal rooms and suites. Some suites have their own names, such as Aarde, Lucht and Lotta, and there is a Royal Suite.

Our pick is a royal room or a suite. Because the rooms were built and renovated in different phases, they vary considerably, and it is in the higher categories that you get what suits a starred evening. Ask about the differences and the view when you book.

The basement houses a pool, a sauna, a steam cabin, a gym and a solarium, and at the Oudtside beauty salon you can book a massage or treatment. Dogs are welcome in a number of specially furnished rooms with wooden floors, for a nightly surcharge, and in the grand café too, but not in the restaurant. For a night with dinner there is the gastronomic package, with the six-course Signature Menu, a night and breakfast.

The story: a family hotel since 1984

Bert and Nelleke van Bourgonje started Merlet in 1984, with eighteen hotel rooms, a pool, a sauna, a restaurant and a meeting room. In 1995 a new restaurant was built, and Grand Café Bourgogne opened in the old one. A year later readers of the ANWB motoring club voted Merlet hotel of the year, and in 1998 came the Michelin star.

In 1999 Carla and Martin van Bourgonje took over the business. Since then Merlet has kept growing, adding suites, new royal rooms, a Royal Suite and meeting rooms, while remaining a family house. 't Oude Raadhuisje also belongs to the hotel, and weddings are held in the Goudenzaal and on the terrace. As far as we are concerned, that is Merlet's strength: a house that has been in the same family for forty years, and you can tell from the way guests are welcomed.

Location and surroundings: things to do around Schoorl and its dunes

Schoorl is a rustic village on the widest dune strip in the Netherlands, and Merlet sits beside the sheep meadow overlooking the Schoorl dunes. The Schoorlse Duinen National Park begins practically at the door, with woodland, heath and open dunes. The Klimduin, the famous drifting dune in the middle of the village, is a classic to scramble up, and through the dunes you can walk or cycle to the beach at Schoorl aan Zee.

For more, drive to the artists' village of Bergen, with its galleries and Museum Kranenburgh, or to Alkmaar, with its canals and, in season, the Friday cheese market. Along the coast to the north lie Groet, Camperduin and the Hondsbossche Duinen. Merlet also organises activities in the area, and the dunes are perfect for exploring by bike.

By car Schoorl is easy to reach from Alkmaar. Schoorl has no railway station; take the train to Alkmaar and then a bus or taxi.

Who is Merlet for?

Merlet is for those who want to combine a starred evening with dunes, sea and a pool, without the price of an Amsterdam grand hotel. It suits walkers and cyclists, couples looking for a weekend on the coast and dog owners, who are welcome in dedicated rooms and the grand café. Vegetarians are taken seriously here too.

If you expect a sleek design hotel with identical new rooms throughout, choose carefully or look elsewhere. And if you want to dine on a Monday, you will find the restaurant closed. Otherwise: a family house with a long track record, and you can taste it.

Good to know

The rooms vary a lot, so choose a royal room or suite deliberately, and note that the restaurant is closed on Mondays.

Frequently asked questions about Merlet

Does Merlet in Schoorl have a Michelin star?

Yes. Restaurant Merlet in Schoorl has held a Michelin star without a break since 1998 and has 17 points from Gault&Millau. The chef is Marco Helsloot. Merlet is a member of Alliance Gastronomique and features in the Lekker list.

How many rooms does Merlet have?

Merlet has 28 rooms, divided into standard rooms, royal rooms and suites, including a Royal Suite. Every room is furnished differently. The basement has a pool, sauna, steam cabin and gym.

What does dinner with an overnight stay at Merlet cost?

For two, we count on about 470 euros for dinner, one night and breakfast at Merlet in Schoorl (midweek, November 2026). That is the gastronomic package with the six-course Signature Menu. Wine comes on top.

Are dogs welcome at Merlet?

Yes. Merlet has a number of specially furnished rooms with wooden floors where dogs are welcome, for a nightly surcharge. Dogs may also join you in Grand Café Bourgogne, but not in the starred restaurant. Let the hotel know in advance.

When is the restaurant at Merlet open?

Restaurant Merlet serves its menus Tuesday to Sunday, and also at lunch on Fridays and Sundays. The restaurant is closed on Mondays; Grand Café Bourgogne is a relaxed alternative on site.

Does Merlet have a vegetarian menu?

Yes. Alongside the Chef's Menu, Restaurant Merlet offers a vegetarian menu that earned four radishes in the Green Guide for its balance and variety of flavours and textures. Allergies and dietary needs are taken into account.
