Our verdict

Dining and staying at Piloersemaborg

When someone asks us where we most love to dine and stay the night, we do not have to think for long. Piloersemaborg. We have been there several times now: in spring, when the trees in front of the borg were still bare, and in summer under full leaf. And every time we drive away with the same feeling. Everything is right here. That is why the borg is number one in this book.

You drive onto the yard and first see the tall stepped gable, with the family crest still set in the wall. The borg dates from 1633, built for Johan de Mepsche. Around 1700 the great barn was added and it became a working farm. It is the last borg farmhouse the province of Groningen has left. Not a castle, then, but a farmhouse with grandeur. Gravel, a big lawn, a small terrace against the wall. It does not feel like arriving at a hotel, more like arriving at family who happen to live in a listed building.

We have slept there in two ways. The first time in a box bed, and to be honest, that is what we came for. A bed built into the wall, behind little doors, the way farmers here slept for centuries. It is snug, it is special, and it is exactly the kind of night you keep talking about at home. The next time we chose a garden room, with its own terrace on the garden side. More space, more light, and in the morning the door wide open. If we had to choose? The box bed for your first visit, the garden room the time after. Really, we just want to do both again.

Then the evening. The restaurant is in the borg itself. White tablecloths, candles on every table, red leather chairs, paintings on the walls and a large chimney piece with a wood stove. Upstairs, under the roof with its sloping walls, there is another dining room, and by candlelight it may be even lovelier. You are not sitting in a restaurant. You are at the home of someone who happens to cook very well.

And Dick Soek does. He has cooked here since 2005 and in 2012 moved his whole restaurant from Leens to the borg. Organic, local, fish from the Wadden Sea and vegetables from the garden. You can see how that reaches the table in the May 2025 menu we took home afterwards. A savoury waffle with chicken liver mousse and porcini powder. A Wadden oyster from the oven with breadcrumbs, parsley and lemon zest. Their own sourdough with farm butter. A Paris-Brest filled with curd and a little vegetable garden. North Sea crab on a cream of roasted carrots. White asparagus with wild garlic anolini and a light morel sauce. Turbot in filo with sorrel sauce. Leg of venison with its jus. And then a choice between a baked pear with Lazuli, a cheese from De Oudwijker in Cothen, or a crisp cinnamon cream with rhubarb. One summer evening we had strawberries with meringue and elderflower. This is not a kitchen that wants to impress with tricks. It is season, flavour and craft, course after course.

And then the part we are most enthusiastic about: the price. Aperitif, wines, water and coffee are simply included in the menu. No puzzling over the wine list, no sums at the end of the evening. They import the wines themselves, and anything you liked you can take home by the bottle at shop prices. For two, dinner, a night and breakfast come to around € 410, with the wine already included. Anyone who has eaten at a starred restaurant and then seen the wine bill knows what that means. For this quality, in this house, it is almost too good to be true. We know of no address in the Netherlands where you get more evening for your money.

What completes it are the people. Dick in the kitchen, Hilda Soek and host and sommelier Theo Mulder in the dining room. You can tell it is their home, and that they are genuinely glad you are there.

Our advice: take a box-bed room on your first visit, walk around the yard before dinner and simply let the wines come. There are only five rooms, so book early. And count on wanting to come back, just like us.

The house: Groningen's last borg farmhouse, from 1633

Piloersemaborg, known locally as the Hamsterborg as well, was built in 1633 for Johan de Mepsche, from a family of Groningen gentry. It did not stay a noble house for long. Around 1700 the borg passed to the Bos family, who built a large barn onto it, and from then on it was a farm. The Wierenga family lived here later. That barn is now the rear house, and it is exactly this mix of manor and working farm that makes the building so rare: of all the borgs the province once had, this is the only borg farmhouse still standing.

The house sits well back behind a broad moat, among lines of tall trees. Goats, geese, chickens and sheep wander the yard, with lambs in spring, and there are horses too. Apples, pears and plums from the orchard end up in the kitchen. The borg, its contents and its land belong to the Stichting Wierenga van Hamsterborg foundation; Dick Soek rents the house and has cooked here since 2005. To us that is the heart of the story: this is not a hotel squeezed into a monument, but a working farmyard where you also happen to eat and sleep extremely well.

Location and surroundings: things to do around Den Ham and Aduard

Den Ham is a hamlet near Aduard, in the Westerkwartier, right in the middle of Middag-Humsterland, the national landscape of terp mounds, winding ditches and villages that have sat on their own little hills for centuries. You see it best from a bicycle saddle. Book the Groninger-oogst package and you get a cycling and a walking route through the area.

Aduard is close by, with the Kloostermuseum on the site of what was once Groningen's largest monastery. Ride or drive on to Niehove, a mound village with a thirteenth-century church and narrow church paths, and to Ezinge, where Museum Wierdenland explains how people lived on their terps here two thousand years ago. Garnwerd on the Reitdiep has the De Meeuw corn mill with the old café Bij Hammingh beside it. Our tip: make a loop through Aduard, Niehove, Ezinge and Garnwerd and have lunch by the water.

The city of Groningen, with the Groninger Museum, is about fifteen minutes away by car. Driving is by far the easiest way to get here: from the city take the N355 towards Aduard, then follow the signs for Oldehove and Den Ham. At the end of the drive the borg is on your right.

Who is Piloersemaborg for?

For anyone who wants an evening where everything is already taken care of. Because aperitif, wine, water and coffee are included in the menu, nobody has to do sums at the end, which makes the borg ideal for a birthday, an anniversary or simply a weekend away together. If you love old houses, farmyards and quiet polder roads, you are in exactly the right place. Dog owners too: dogs are welcome if you mention it in advance, and you can even bring your own horse.

It suits you less if you want a hotel with a reception desk, a spa or room service. There are five rooms and beyond that mostly peace and pasture. The garden rooms have twin beds, and a box bed is a bed built into the wall behind little doors: you have to like the idea, though we know few people who are disappointed. For us this has been the best address in the province of Groningen for years, and number one in the whole book.

Good to know

There are only five rooms and they can only be booked with dinner; the borg is closed on Mondays and Tuesdays.

Frequently asked questions about Piloersemaborg

Can you dine at Piloersemaborg without staying the night?

Yes. The restaurant at Piloersemaborg is open to diners who are not staying over. From Thursday to Saturday evening you choose between a four or six course menu, and on Sunday afternoon there is a four course lunch menu. Aperitif, wine, water and coffee are always included. It does not work the other way round: rooms can only be booked together with dinner.

Does Piloersemaborg have a Michelin star or Gault&Millau points?

Piloersemaborg does not have a Michelin star. Gault&Millau gives the restaurant 14 points, and it is number 71 in the Lekker500. Chef Dick Soek did hold a star before, at Schathoes Verhildersum in Leens. When he moved his restaurant, staff and all, to Den Ham in 2012, the star lapsed.

How many rooms does Piloersemaborg have?

Piloersemaborg has five rooms: three garden rooms and two box-bed rooms. The garden rooms have a sitting room with French doors to a private terrace, a separate bedroom with twin beds and a shower. The box-bed rooms are upstairs in the middle section, with a box bed of 2.10 by 1.60 metres, a sitting area, a shower and a private terrace downstairs.

What does dinner with an overnight stay at Piloersemaborg cost for two?

We reckon on around 410 euros for two for dinner, one night and breakfast, wine included (midweek, November 2026). That is the package with a night in a box-bed room, a regional breakfast and an all-in four course menu, so with aperitif, wines, water and coffee. Choose six courses or a garden room and you pay a little extra.

Are dogs welcome at Piloersemaborg?

Yes, dogs are welcome at Piloersemaborg, but do let them know in advance. Unusually, you can bring your own horse as well: it can be stabled in the barns, and there is a riding arena behind the rear house. Bear in mind that goats, geese, chickens and sheep roam the yard.

On which days is Piloersemaborg open?

According to its own website, Piloersemaborg cooks from Thursday to Saturday evening, with a choice of four or six courses, and serves a four course lunch on Sunday afternoon. Monday and Tuesday are closing days. The calendar on the website shows which nights are still free for each room type, and with five rooms it pays to look early.

What is there to do near Piloersemaborg?

Piloersemaborg lies in Middag-Humsterland, a landscape of terp mounds and winding ditches. Nearby are the Kloostermuseum in Aduard, the mound village of Niehove, Museum Wierdenland in Ezinge and Garnwerd on the Reitdiep. The Groninger-oogst package includes a cycling and walking route through the area, and the city of Groningen is fifteen minutes away by car.
